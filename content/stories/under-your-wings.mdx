# Under Your Wings

## The Song

There are seasons when anxiety doesn't arrive as a single dramatic event — it settles in slowly, like a change in atmospheric pressure. This song came from one of those seasons. Not a crisis with a clear beginning and end, but a prolonged stretch of feeling exposed, unsteady, and worn down by the effort of staying guarded. The kind of fatigue that comes not from doing too much, but from bracing too long.

What surprised me in writing it was how honest the need felt. Not noble, not spiritual in any polished sense — just a raw desire to stop standing watch over myself and rest somewhere safe. That desire led me, almost against my instincts, to prayer. And in prayer, to a posture I hadn't expected: not asking for the anxiety to be removed, but asking to be held inside it. To find shelter not on the other side of fear, but within it.

That shift is what the song is about. "Under Your Wings" doesn't promise that the threat disappears. It describes what it's like to stop fighting for control and trust something — someone — larger than your own vigilance to hold the perimeter. That's not passivity. It's a different kind of courage.

---

## The Sound

The arrangement was built around the physical sensation of being covered — not concealed, but protected. Soft harmonic layers open slowly and stay open, the way a hand rests steady without gripping. There's almost no rhythmic urgency in the production; the pacing resists the pull toward tension and resolution that most music relies on to create momentum. Instead, the music holds still and lets you settle into it. That was a deliberate choice, because the spiritual reality the song is trying to describe is not dramatic. It's quiet. It's nearness.

The sustained pads and unhurried movement aren't decoration — they're the argument. Music about rest should sound like rest is possible. If the arrangement had pushed and climbed, the lyrics would have fought against it. The gentleness is the theology.

---

## The Scripture

Psalm 91 is one of the most striking passages in the Old Testament. It opens with an image: whoever dwells in the shelter of the Most High rests in the shadow of the Almighty. Then it goes further — God as refuge, as fortress, as the one whose wings provide cover from what threatens. Psalm 57 adds a related image: "I will take refuge in the shadow of your wings until the disaster has passed." What's notable in both texts is the specificity. This isn't abstract comfort — it's shelter in the middle of real danger. The psalmist isn't pretending the threat isn't there. The threat is named. The refuge is named alongside it. The claim is that God's care is present and immediate, not distant and conditional.

---

## Reflection

The hardest part of this song to write — and to mean — was releasing the assumption that vigilance and faith are the same thing. That staying alert and staying safe are the same thing. For a long time, I confused them.

Many people carry a version of this tension: the sense that if you stop monitoring the horizon, something will go wrong, and that will be your fault for resting. "Under Your Wings" is an attempt to name that exhaustion honestly and offer a different frame — one where safety is found not in effort but in trust. Whether or not you share the theological conviction, the question the song asks is a genuine one:

**What would it cost you to stop being your own guardian for a while — and what, or who, would you need to trust instead?**

## For Worship Leaders

This song works well in contexts where the congregation needs permission to stop striving — moments of prayer, response, or quiet after a heavier teaching. Its lack of rhythmic urgency makes it an effective anchor for extended moments of stillness rather than a call-to-action song. The imagery is pastoral and scriptural, rooted in Psalm 91, making it theologically straightforward to introduce without extensive framing.
