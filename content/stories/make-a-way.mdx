# Make a Way — Song Story

---

## The Song

Some seasons don't announce themselves as difficult. They arrive quietly, dressed as ordinary life, and it's only after several months that you recognize what's been happening: you've been waiting. Not for anything dramatic, necessarily — sometimes just for clarity. For one door to open, or one answer to arrive, or one sign that the path you're standing on actually leads somewhere.

"Make a Way" was written inside that kind of season. Forward movement had stalled. Prayers were being offered honestly and regularly, and the silence on the other end was not hostile — but it was real. What emerged from that place wasn't a song of resolution. It was something closer to a reckoning with dependence. The title itself is borrowed from the posture of the prayer: *make a way*, because left to my own navigation, I cannot see one.

What surprised me in writing it was discovering that the request — *make a way* — wasn't primarily about circumstances changing. It became, through the writing process, something more fundamental. A confession that I am not the one holding this together. That faithfulness in the waiting matters as much as the outcome the waiting is for. The song doesn't end with a breakthrough. It ends with trust still forming, which felt like the most honest thing to write.

---

## The Sound

The arrangement was built to feel like patience rather than perform it. Steady, grounding rhythms anchor the piece without urgency — there is no sense that the music is rushing toward a climax. The harmonic layers warm slowly, the way trust actually forms: not in a single moment of clarity, but through accumulated steadiness over time.

Dynamic movement is present, but it is subtle. What builds in this song is not tension toward release — it is weight, gathered gradually, the way conviction deepens rather than ignites. The instrumentation was chosen to support someone sitting inside uncertainty, not to pull them out of it prematurely. If the music communicates anything, it is this: the quiet work God does in a person during the long stretches between visible answers is real work, and it deserves to sound like something.

---

## The Scripture

Isaiah 43:19 reads: *"See, I am doing a new thing. Now it springs up — do you not perceive it? I am making a way in the wilderness and streams in the wasteland."* The image is striking because it doesn't describe a comfortable path. Wilderness and wasteland are honest words. The promise is not that the terrain becomes easy, but that provision appears within it — water in a place where water has no business being.

Proverbs 3:5–6 puts the human side of the equation plainly: *"Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways submit to him, and he will make your paths straight."* The phrase "lean not on your own understanding" is not anti-intellectual. It is a recognition that the map in your hands may not show what God's perspective already sees. Submission here is not passivity — it is the active choice to stop treating your own limited vantage point as the final word on what is possible.

---

## Reflection

Waiting is uncomfortable enough on its own, but what often makes it harder is the suspicion that it might mean something went wrong — that the silence is an answer in itself. This song pushes back on that. Not by pretending the discomfort isn't real, but by locating trust in something other than favorable conditions.

Here is a question worth sitting with: **What would it mean for you to trust something — or someone — not because the circumstances are encouraging, but because the character of that something has proven reliable before?**
